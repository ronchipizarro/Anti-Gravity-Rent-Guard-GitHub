## IMPORTANT LEGAL NOTES

1. **Counting the 3 days:** The 3-day period begins the day after service and excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays (FL Stat. 83.56(4)).

2. **Content requirements:** Under Florida law, the notice must state the specific amount of rent due and the specific rental period for which it is owed. Do NOT include late fees, utilities, or other charges — only the rent amount specified in the lease.

3. **Do NOT include demands for non-rent amounts.** Florida courts have invalidated 3-day notices that demand payment of amounts other than rent (utilities, late fees, damages). If the notice demands anything beyond rent, it may be deemed defective and the entire eviction case could be dismissed.

4. **Lease definition of "rent."** If the lease specifically defines certain charges as "additional rent" (e.g., parking, pet fees), consult with the attorney about whether these may be included. When in doubt, include only base rent.

5. **Service must be proper.** Improper service is the #1 reason eviction cases are dismissed or delayed. Always document the method of service with photos, timestamps, and/or certified mail receipts.

6. **Rent Guard serves via POA.** Rent Guard serves this notice in the landlord's name under the Irrevocable Power of Attorney (Protection Agreement Art. VI). The notice is FROM the landlord, served BY Rent Guard as agent.
